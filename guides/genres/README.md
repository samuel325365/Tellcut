# Video genres

Reference for writing a storyboard (step 3 of "Steps for a new film" in `guides/workflow.md`).

How to use:
1. Pick the one genre below that fits the film (by what the footage is and what the film is for).
2. Read that genre's card, `guides/genres/<id>.md`.
3. The storyboard answers each **must-have** one by one (where and how it is done; if it cannot be, say why), avoids the **pitfalls**, and uses extras where they help.
4. Pick the editing style from those the card recommends (in order of fit). A film usually combines styles from different axes: one layout + one caption style + one look, then a pace.
5. For the layout, read the card's line for the film's ratio and that ratio's row in "Ratios" below.

Card fields: ratios (first = main), typical length, face (whether a real person must be on camera), production (whether it can be made without filming).
Length classes: short = under 60 s, mid = 1–5 min, long = over 5 min.

## Genres

### Repurposing: cut a new video out of existing long content

- [`clip`](clip.md) — Long-form clipping — Pick the most gripping 30–90 s of a long video, to send viewers back to the original. — 20–90 s
- [`podcast`](podcast.md) — Podcast & interview clip — A kind of long-form clip: a conversation between two or more people; viewers must always know who is talking. — 30 s–3 min
- [`stream`](stream.md) — Stream highlight — Cut one event out of a live stream, with just enough lead-up that people who missed the stream get it. — 20 s–3 min
- [`highlight`](highlight.md) — Gameplay / sports highlight — One key moment, plus the build-up and replays that make it hit. — 10–60 s (compilations up to 10 min)
- [`recap`](recap.md) — Movie / series recap — Narration-led: compress the plot of a film or a season into a few minutes. — 3–15 min (short version 60 s)
- [`react`](react.md) — Reaction & commentary — The original video fills the main frame; you react and comment from a corner. — 1–20 min
- [`news`](news.md) — News explainer — Sum up an event quickly after it happens, with your own take. — 45 s–5 min

### Education: the viewer learns one thing

- [`explainer`](explainer.md) — Animated explainer — One video explains one concept clearly, with graphics and animation instead of filming. — 45 s–10 min
- [`listicle`](listicle.md) — Top-N list — "5 things…" counting down to number one; fixed structure, easy to template. — 30 s–8 min
- [`myth`](myth.md) — Myth busting — Start from a wrong common belief, then flip it with evidence. — 20–60 s
- [`tutorial`](tutorial.md) — How-to / screen tutorial — Teach the viewer to do one concrete thing, usually a screen recording or hands doing it. — 30 s–20 min
- [`dataviz`](dataviz.md) — Data visualization — Answer one question with animated charts, e.g. a bar-chart race or a changing map. — 30 s–5 min
- [`story`](story.md) — Narrated storytelling — Tell a history, mystery or urban-legend story in voice-over; no face needed. — 1–30 min
- [`course`](course.md) — Course & lecture — Teach a whole topic systematically, usually as part of a series. — 10–60 min

### Real life: record what really happened

- [`montage`](montage.md) — Highlight montage — Condense an experience (wedding, trip, event) with music driving the emotion. — 30 s–4 min
- [`vlog`](vlog.md) — Vlog / day in my life — A stretch of time strung together from the main character's point of view; viewers watch the person and the lifestyle. — 1–20 min
- [`travel`](travel.md) — Travel — Take viewers to a place: informative (a guide) or atmospheric (cinematic). — 30 s–20 min
- [`food`](food.md) — Food (review / recipe) — A restaurant visit or a recipe; it has to look delicious. — 20 s–10 min
- [`outfit`](outfit.md) — Outfit / GRWM transition — Get ready with me or a one-second outfit change; wins on transitions and the beat. — 10–45 s
- [`makeover`](makeover.md) — Before & after — Show the bad starting point first, reveal the result at the end. — 20 s–10 min
- [`pet`](pet.md) — Pets — Animal expressions and behaviour, often with an "inner voice" written for them. — 10–45 s

### Entertainment & drama: make people laugh and want to share

- [`skit`](skit.md) — Skit / POV — One situation, one twist, often one person playing several roles. — 15–90 s
- [`meme`](meme.md) — Meme / trend — Take a trending sound or template and make your own version. — 5–30 s
- [`street`](street.md) — Street interview — Ask strangers on the street the same question and keep the funniest answers. — 30 s–10 min
- [`challenge`](challenge.md) — Challenge — Set a rule and see whether it can be done. — 30 s–20 min
- [`shortfilm`](shortfilm.md) — Short film — A fully scripted narrative piece; acting, cinematography and sound design matter. — 3–30 min
- [`mv`](mv.md) — Music video — The picture follows one song entirely, in service of the song. — 2–5 min

### Sensory & soothing: pleasant to watch, relaxing to hear

- [`asmr`](asmr.md) — ASMR — Sound is the subject, bringing relaxation or tingles. — 30 s–1 h
- [`satisfying`](satisfying.md) — Satisfying / process timelapse — Show a whole process: cleaning, restoring, crafting, assembling. — 15 s–10 min
- [`ambient`](ambient.md) — Ambient loop — Picture plus music you can leave running, e.g. lo-fi, a rainy window. — 10 s loop – several hours

### Business & brand: the end goal is conversion or trust

- [`ugc`](ugc.md) — UGC ad — A product recommended in an ordinary person's voice, looking like sharing, not advertising. — 15–60 s
- [`review`](review.md) — Unboxing & review — Unbox, test, give pros and cons and who it is for. — 45 s–20 min
- [`talking`](talking.md) — Talking head — Opinions spoken to camera; viewers follow the person. — 30 s–15 min
- [`brand`](brand.md) — Brand film — Tell a story about people and values, with restrained branding. — 1–5 min
- [`tvc`](tvc.md) — Product commercial — Make people remember one product and one selling point in very little time. — 6, 15 or 30 s

### Templates & interaction: fixed structure, the easiest to mass-produce in code

- [`quiz`](quiz.md) — Quiz — Question, countdown, reveal: viewers answer as they watch. — 30 s–10 min
- [`textstory`](textstory.md) — Text story + gameplay — Story captions and voice on the top half, constantly moving gameplay on the bottom half. — 45 s–3 min
- [`kinetic`](kinetic.md) — Kinetic typography — The text itself is the star, appearing with the rhythm of music or speech. — 10–60 s

### Live events & keepsakes: played on a big screen or at a particular occasion

- [`opener`](opener.md) — Event opener — Played as an event begins, to lift the room. — 30 s–2 min
- [`growth`](growth.md) — Growth & love story — Played at a wedding banquet: the couple's photos from childhood on tell how they met. — 3–6 min
- [`memorial`](memorial.md) — Memorial film — Played at a funeral or memorial, looking back on a life. — 3–8 min
- [`loopwall`](loopwall.md) — Display loop — Replayed endlessly at a booth, shop or lobby; people pass by and leave at any moment. — 15 s–3 min loop
- [`yearrecap`](yearrecap.md) — Year in review — Look back on the key moments of a year (personal, team, company). — 1–5 min

## Editing styles

Axes: pace (how fast, speed changes), text (how captions and text appear), layout (how the frame is split), look (grade, texture, composition), format (a whole fixed approach). Styles on different axes stack, e.g. split + hormozi + telop. Most sources are editing-tool vendor blogs; numbers like "a cut every 2–3 s" are industry observation, not statistics.

| id | axis | name (reference) | traits | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `retention` | pace | Retention editing (MrBeast (before 2024)) | Rhythm: a new shot or angle every 2–3 s, no dead air. Text: big titles, key words enlarged. Look: high saturation. Sound: lots of SFX, flash effects. | In March 2024 MrBeast said this overstimulating pace no longer works and moved to slower, story-led editing with room to breathe between scenes. Fine for shorts; hold back in long videos. |
| `velocity` | pace | Velocity / beat sync (popular CapCut templates) | Rhythm: cut on the beat; speed up before the downbeat, slow down on it (speed ramp). Text: little or no text. Look: motion blur, white-flash transitions. Sound: driven by strongly rhythmic music. | Do not hold slow motion too long: a long "cinematic" slow shot loses viewers. |
| `hormozi` | captions & text | Hormozi-style captions (Alex Hormozi) | Rhythm: jump cuts about every 1–3 s, punch in on key points. Text: bold caps, 2–4 words at a time, the spoken word turns yellow or green, key words enlarged. Look: dark background, speaker centred. Sound: talking-led. | The most common caption style for talking shorts today. Chinese versions usually show 4–8 characters at a time with key words coloured. |
| `telop` | captions & text | Variety telop (Japanese, Korean and Taiwanese variety shows) | Rhythm: cut to the jokes and reactions. Text: coloured, shaped, animated text cards, often voicing the quip the person on screen did not say. Look: bright. Sound: SFX that land the jokes. | Heavily used in Japanese variety TV since the 1990s; research says it is not only for comprehension but for making jokes land and keeping viewers. |
| `kinetic` | captions & text | Kinetic typography (lyric videos, quote videos) | Rhythm: text appears with the voice or the beat. Text: the text is the star: either clean sans-serif, or expressive handwriting, neon, glitch type. Look: simple background. Sound: music or narration. | Research splits lyric-video type into functional (clear, legible) and artistic (the face itself carries the emotion). |
| `split` | layout | Split / stacked layout (default layout of podcast-clipping tools) | Rhythm: switch with whoever is talking, not on a fixed timer. Text: captions labelled with the speaker name. Look: stacked top/bottom (vertical) or side by side (wide). Sound: the conversation's own sound. | When the source is a wide two-shot, a straight 9:16 crop often cuts both people; track the speaker and reframe. |
| `pip` | layout | Reaction / duet (TikTok duets, reaction videos) | Rhythm: follow the source; pause it to insert comments. Text: comment captions. Look: source large plus a face box, or duet stacked top/bottom. Sound: source audio ducked, commentary on top. | Listed among the most common short-form formats. |
| `greenscreen` | layout | Greenscreen explain (the approach Tom Scott pioneered) | Rhythm: background material changes every 4–6 s. Text: little. Look: speaker in the left or right third, background is screenshots or news footage. Sound: clean voice, very little music. | For talking over material you did not shoot, e.g. news explainers. |
| `splitstory` | layout | Story over gameplay (Reddit stories over Minecraft parkour) | Rhythm: the lower half keeps flowing without a break. Text: word-by-word highlighted captions. Look: story on top, smooth HUD-free gameplay below. Sound: TTS or voice-over narration. | The claim "narration holds verbal attention, motion holds visual attention" is only a hypothesis; vendor retention numbers have no research behind them. |
| `cinematic` | look & grade | Phone-first cinematic (travel and food creators) | Rhythm: slow moves on a tripod or gimbal. Text: little. Look: one LUT for the whole film, light grain; teal-orange is common. Sound: music plus ambience. | For subjects that already look good: places, people, food. Reels favours polished grading more than TikTok. |
| `docreal` | look & grade | Documentary realism (high-production creators) | Rhythm: cinematic camera moves, slow push-ins, a 24 fps feel. Text: only when needed. Look: one restrained grade throughout. Sound: ambience and score. | Needs better shooting quality than others in the genre to carry it. |
| `retro` | look & grade | Retro digicam / DV (Gen Z digital-camera revival) | Rhythm: casual. Text: date stamp at the bottom. Look: warm, grainy, hazy, dark yet blown out. Sound: live sound. | NPR (2026): the trend spread from TikTok to Instagram; Canon PowerShot sales grew almost 7× from 2022 to 2025. |
| `pastel` | look & grade | Symmetry & pastel (Wes Anderson trend) (2023 TikTok Wes Anderson trend) | Rhythm: quick cuts between symmetrical shots. Text: centred title cards. Look: strict symmetry, pastel palette, deadpan faces. Sound: light orchestral music. | Started from a train-commute video (13M+ views) that made everyday life look like a fairy tale. |
| `flatlay` | look & grade | Aesthetic flat-lay (outfits, skincare, desks, unboxing) | Rhythm: items placed one by one, or a slow pan. Text: little. Look: straight top-down or 45°, pastel or warm amber, soft natural light. Sound: sounds of placing things, or soft music. | Works better on Reels and Pinterest than on TikTok. |
| `cleanbroll` | whole format | Polished talking head + B-roll (Ali Abdaal) | Rhythm: fast, lots of B-roll. Text: titles and motion graphics. Look: clean, polished. Sound: voice plus light music. | Adobe reports about 30 h of editing per video, 8 h of it finding B-roll and 6 h on titles and animation. |
| `statichead` | whole format | Talking-head static (Leila Hormozi and others) | Rhythm: one shot, one angle, no cuts. Text: captions in chunks, or none. Look: clean, warm neutral. Sound: voice only. | For creators who are the brand, whose viewers care about the opinion, not the production. |
| `brainrot` | whole format | Brainrot stack (information-dense shorts) | Rhythm: a cut every 0.6–0.8 s. Text: white text with coloured highlights, popping in line by line. Look: 2–3 stacked layers (talking + gameplay + other). Sound: an ASMR sound layer under the voice. | Built to trap attention; risks looking cheap and clashing with a brand. |
| `essay` | whole format | Video essay (Vox / Johnny Harris) (Vox, Johnny Harris) | Rhythm: a chart or map every 8–10 s, slower pace. Text: one sans-serif face, lower-third title bars. Look: animated maps, paper texture, film grain. Sound: narration up front, music low; paper and typewriter sounds. | For explaining a system, a piece of history, a phenomenon. |
| `flatanim` | whole format | Flat animated explainer (Kurzgesagt) | Rhythm: narration-driven, the picture always moving. Text: a few labels. Look: vector illustration, rounded shapes, bright palette, 2D with a little 3D. Sound: a score written for each video. | Strip the details so only the core mechanism shows; cute characters make it recognisable. |
| `doodle` | whole format | Whiteboard / stick figure (faceless explainer channels) | Rhythm: drawn stroke by stroke with the narration. Text: handwriting. Look: whiteboard doodles, stick figures, hand-drawn lines. Sound: narration-led. | Stick figures suit light explainers and humour; hand drawing suits personal stories. |
| `silentvlog` | whole format | Silent vlog (Korean café vlogs) | Rhythm: slow, everyday pace. Text: nobody speaks; captions are the narration. Look: warm, often faceless. Sound: soft music plus ASMR of cooking, cleaning. | YouTube's trends blog names South Korea as the main origin; café vlogs grew fast from late 2019. |
| `hands` | whole format | Hands-only ASMR (restoration, crafts, "boring work") | Rhythm: real time or slightly sped up. Text: no captions. Look: top-down or close-up, only the hands. Sound: no music, just the clean sound of the work. | Bookbinding and restoration channels are the classic examples. |
| `ugcnative` | whole format | Native phone-shot UGC (beauty, wellness and e-commerce ads) | Rhythm: handheld, casual; the product appears mid-video. Text: short reaction captions ("ok wait", "obsessed"). Look: natural light, lived-in rooms, slight shake. Sound: spoken, not read from a script. | Analysts say phone-shot video is beating studio content and conversation is replacing scripts. |
| `pov` | whole format | POV scenario (situational skits) | Rhythm: payoff within about 30 s. Text: speaks to the viewer: "you turn around and see…". Look: first-person view, props reaching in from the bottom of the frame. Sound: situational SFX. |  |
| `photodump` | whole format | Photo dump (IG photo dumps, CapCut photo templates) | Rhythm: 5–20 photos, 0.5–2.5 s each, on the beat. Text: little. Look: deliberately unpolished, casual. Sound: trending music. | Strongest photo first, the most conclusive one last. |
| `audioreactive` | whole format | Audio-reactive loop (creators with original sound) | Rhythm: 8–30 s loop, no narrative. Text: none. Look: one subject that morphs and changes colour on audio peaks. Sound: the picture is driven entirely by the sound. | For autoplaying, rewatched loop content. |

## Ratios

"Default safe area" is the margin `theme.safe` gives the shared blocks by default (`defaultSafe` in the engine; change it with `c.theme({ safe })`), in pixels at the listed size and as a fraction of the frame. Platform UI positions change between app versions; numbers are approximate.

| ratio | size | where it is posted | what covers the frame | default safe area (top / bottom / each side) | tip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16:9 | 1920×1080 | YouTube long-form, TV, projection, Bilibili, wedding and event screens, LinkedIn | TV treats the central 90% as title-safe; on YouTube the bottom ~12% is covered by the progress bar and captions. | 65 / 130 / 96 px (6% / 12% / 5%) | Best for several windows, side-by-side comparisons, wide views. Also the best master: centre crops give 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16. |
| 9:16 | 1080×1920 | YouTube Shorts, IG Reels, TikTok, Stories, Douyin, Xiaohongshu | Account name over the top ~220 px, caption and music info over the bottom ~420 px, like/comment buttons over the right ~130 px. Keep key content in the middle, slightly high. | 221 / 422 / 130 px (11.5% / 22% / 12%) | One subject per frame. Stack several people vertically; put captions in the lower middle, clear of the bottom UI. |
| 1:1 | 1080×1080 | IG/FB feed, LinkedIn, X, ad placements | Almost nothing covers it. It appears in a feed one post after another, so it must read at first glance. | 65 / 86 / 65 px (6% / 8% / 6%) | Fits every platform without cropping. Common layout: title bar on top, video in the middle, captions below. |
| 4:5 | 1080×1350 | IG/FB feed (largest vertical size there), the most common ad size | Takes the most screen height in a feed without the Reels UI covering it. | 81 / 108 / 65 px (6% / 8% / 6%) | The usual way to put a 9:16 video into a feed is to crop it to 4:5. |
| 21:9 | 2560×1080 (or 16:9 with black bars) | Cinematic shorts, brand films, wide exhibition screens, title sequences | On a normal 16:9 screen it shows black bars top and bottom; captions can sit in the bars. | 65 / 130 / 128 px (6% / 12% / 5%) | The bars themselves say "this is a film". Good for emotional pieces, not for information-dense content. |
| 4:3 | 1440×1080 | Retro, VHS or home-video looks, some projectors and old TVs | In a 16:9 player it shows side bars; fill them with a blurred, enlarged copy of the picture. | 65 / 130 / 72 px (6% / 12% / 5%) | The ratio itself says "another era": good for memories and growing-up films. |

**16:9 as a master for several ratios**: keep the subject and important text inside the central area common to all crops, then crop 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 from the same master instead of laying each out again. Where this fails: side-by-side people (e.g. a two-window podcast) always get cut at 9:16; lay those out again, stacked.
